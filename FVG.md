## 1. Core Definition of FVG

**Fair Value Gap (FVG)** is a 3-candle price imbalance where the middle candle's wick does NOT overlap with the candles on either side, creating a "gap" or inefficiency in price. This represents an area where price moved so fast that normal buying/selling did not occur -- an imbalance.

- FVG appears when strong momentum causes price to skip levels.
- Smart Money typically returns to fill these gaps (rebalance).
- FVGs act as support/resistance and optimal entry zones.
- Also referred to as: **Imbalance**, **Inefficiency**, **Price Void**, **Liquidity Void**.

**Source:** Video 13 (hMb-cEAVKcQ) - Primary dedicated FVG video.

**Underlying Mechanics (from V13):**
- **Imbalance** = unequal buy/sell pressure causing rapid price movement.
- **Inefficiency** = price levels that were skipped due to imbalance, creating "unfair" pricing where normal market participants did not get to transact.
- Market seeks efficiency -- tends to return to these zones to allow proper price discovery.
- FVG, Order Blocks, and Breaker Blocks all represent various forms of imbalance/inefficiency that market aims to rebalance.

## 3. Bullish FVG vs Bearish FVG

### Bullish FVG (Demand Imbalance)
- **Formation:** During strong upward move when price gaps up leaving inefficiency below.
- **3-Candle Pattern:** Middle bullish candle's low wick does not touch first candle's high or third candle's low.
- **FVG Zone:** Gap between first candle's HIGH and third candle's LOW.
- **Location:** Below current price.
- **Function:** Acts as SUPPORT.
- **Trading Action:** Buy when price returns to fill this gap during pullback.
- **SM Behavior:** Smart Money places buy orders in Bullish FVG zones.

### Bearish FVG (Supply Imbalance)
- **Formation:** During strong downward move when price gaps down leaving inefficiency above.
- **3-Candle Pattern:** Middle bearish candle's high wick does not touch first candle's low or third candle's high.
- **FVG Zone:** Gap between first candle's LOW and third candle's HIGH.
- **Location:** Above current price.
- **Function:** Acts as RESISTANCE.
- **Trading Action:** Sell when price returns to fill this gap during retracement.
- **SM Behavior:** Smart Money places sell orders in Bearish FVG zones.

## 4. FVG Formation Rules (3-Candle Pattern)

### Identification Pattern
```
Candle 1    Candle 2 (Impulse)    Candle 3
  |              |                    |
  | high ---     |                    |
  |         \    |                    |
  |          ----+-- NO OVERLAP --+---| low
  |              |   (THIS IS     |   |
  |              |    THE FVG)    |   |
  |              |                |   |
```

### Rules:
1. **Three consecutive candles** are required.
2. The **middle candle** must be the impulse candle (strong momentum).
3. The middle candle's **wick must NOT overlap** with the candles on either side.
4. The gap between Candle 1's extreme and Candle 3's extreme = FVG zone.
5. **Bullish FVG:** Gap between C1 high and C3 low (C2 is bullish impulse).
6. **Bearish FVG:** Gap between C1 low and C3 high (C2 is bearish impulse).
7. FVG represents a **price inefficiency** -- levels where normal two-sided trading did not occur.

## 5. FVG Fill / Rebalance (70-80% Fill Rate)

**From Video 13 (hMb-cEAVKcQ):**

- **FVG Fill (Rebalance)** occurs when price returns to the Fair Value Gap zone to fill the inefficiency.
- Market has a natural tendency to rebalance -- **70-80% of FVGs get filled eventually**.
- **Full fill** = price completely fills the gap (closes the entire zone).
- **Partial fill** = price enters FVG but does not fill completely.
- **Unfilled FVG** remains as strong support/resistance until eventually tested.
- When FVG fills, it often provides excellent entry with tight stop loss above/below the FVG.
- The fill represents **Smart Money re-entering** or **institutional order absorption**.

### Fill Lifecycle:
```
FRESH FVG --> Price approaches --> Partial Fill --> Full Fill --> Rebalanced
     |              |                  |              |             |
  Strongest     Still valid       Reduced        Completed    Zone spent
  S/R power     for entry        but active     (used up)    (ignore)
```

### Key Statistics:
- 70-80% fill rate (high probability of return)
- Time to fill varies: minutes to days depending on timeframe
- Unfilled FVGs on higher timeframes remain significant longer

## 6. FVG as Entry Zone (When Price Pulls Back to Fill)

**Primary Entry Logic:**
1. FVG forms during an impulse move (strong directional candle).
2. Price continues in the impulse direction initially.
3. Price eventually retraces/pulls back.
4. When price reaches the FVG zone, this is the **optimal entry point**.
5. The FVG zone provides a **precise entry area** with logical stop placement.

**Entry Mechanics (from V13):**
- **Bullish trend entry:** Price pulls back to Bullish FVG zone --> enter LONG.
- **Bearish trend entry:** Price retraces to Bearish FVG zone --> enter SHORT.
- **Stop loss:** Beyond the FVG zone boundary (below for longs, above for shorts).
- **Tight risk:** FVG zones are typically narrow, allowing tight stop losses.
- **High R:R:** Tight stop + structural target = excellent risk/reward.

**Why FVG Works as Entry:**
- Represents institutional order absorption zone.
- Market "needs" to rebalance this inefficiency.
- Smart Money strategically places orders in FVG zones.
- Price acceptance in FVG validates the rebalance and confirms continuation.

## 8. How FVG Connects to Other Concepts

### FVG + Impulse Moves
- FVGs are **created BY** impulse moves (strong directional candles).
- The stronger the impulse, the larger the FVG.
- Impulse moves that create BOS often leave FVGs behind.
- From V4: Impulse swings (no pullback) create conditions for FVG formation.

### FVG + Pullback
- FVG is the **destination** of pullbacks -- price pulls back to fill the FVG.
- Valid pullback (V3) often terminates at an FVG zone.
- Pullback + FVG fill = high probability entry point.
- 70-80% fill rate means pullbacks reliably target FVGs.

### FVG + Order Block (OB)
- **Key Confluence (V14):** When FVG and OB overlap, this creates the highest probability entry zone.
- The OB (last candle before BOS) often has an FVG immediately adjacent or overlapping.
- OB provides the "why" (institutional orders), FVG provides the "where" (precise price level).
- Combined OB+FVG entry = tight stop, clear zone, institutional backing.
- From V14: "Combine with FVG, inducement, premium/discount for confluence."

### FVG + Premium/Discount Zones
- **From V12 + V13:** FVG in the correct zone amplifies probability.
- Bullish FVG in **discount zone** (lower 50%) = highest probability long entry.
- Bearish FVG in **premium zone** (upper 50%) = highest probability short entry.
- FVG in wrong zone (e.g., bullish FVG in premium) = lower probability, may skip.
- From V15 checklist: "Entry zone in discount (bull) or premium (bear)."

### FVG + Million Dollar Setup (V15)
- FVG is one of the **7 required elements** of the Million Dollar Setup.
- Checklist item #4: "Fair Value Gap present."
- Maximum confluence: Trend + CHoCH + OB + **FVG** + Premium/Discount + Inducement.
- FVG provides the **precise entry zone** within the broader setup.
- Probability scaling: single concept ~30-40%, Million Dollar Setup with FVG = 80-90%.

### FVG + Inducement (V4 connection)
- From V4 (Inducement Shift Rule 2): "Liquidity void or Fair Value Gap may require deeper sweep."
- Price imbalances (FVGs) influence which inducement levels get swept.
- Deeper OBs with FVGs attract sweeps of multiple inducement levels.
- FVG acts as a "magnet" pulling price deeper into pullback zones.

### FVG + BOS
- FVGs typically form during BOS impulse candles.
- The BOS candle itself often creates the FVG.
- After BOS, price leaves FVG behind as it breaks structure.
- Pullback to FVG after BOS = entry in direction of new structure.

### FVG + Sessions/Kill Zones (V16)
- From V16: Mark FVGs during Asian session preparation.
- Execute FVG entries during London/NY kill zones (peak liquidity).
- FVGs formed during kill zones have higher significance (more institutional volume).
